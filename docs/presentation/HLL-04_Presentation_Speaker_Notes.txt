SLIDE 2: The data collection problem
Target: 45 seconds. Main talk target 770 seconds (12 minutes 50 seconds).

Consider a basic workflow where a person copies photos into a folder and types descriptions into a spreadsheet. Each row must continue to refer to the intended image. Missing fields, mistaken filenames and plausible but incorrect descriptions are different failure modes. These are illustrative risks; I have not measured their frequency in a company. The proposed interface brings image import, metadata entry, review and export together. That can make certain mistakes easier to prevent or recover from, but it also introduces a cost: more structure may take longer or feel restrictive. The project must evaluate both the data and the work needed to produce it.

SLIDE 5: Human evidence behind the design
Target: 60 seconds. Main talk target 770 seconds (12 minutes 50 seconds).

Three human studies support related design choices. A study with 65 participants tested bundled form improvements and found fewer submission attempts. Two of three forms also showed significantly shorter completion times. Another study with 166 participants found that explicit format rules before entry reduced errors and attempts. A study with 303 participants found better performance when errors appeared beside the affected field. These findings support clear rules and local feedback in the guided interface. They concern other tasks and implementations, so the project's own comparison must establish whether the benefits transfer.

Additional panel answers
These are results from other researchers' human studies. Seckler and colleagues tested bundled web-form improvements with 65 participants in two groups. For two of three reconstructed registration forms, mean completion time fell from 113 to 85 seconds and from 105 to 70 seconds, with significant differences. The third time difference was not significant. Submission attempts decreased for all three forms. This supports testing the combined design rather than assigning the gains to a single control.

The 2011 format study involved 166 participants. Advance format specifications reduced errors and submission attempts. Explicit rules were the most efficient approach, and adding an example did not provide a further benefit. The 2012 location study involved 303 participants and six error-message positions. Messages near the erroneous field performed better than messages at the top or bottom. The right side was most satisfying and intuitive. Location evidence does not establish when a message should appear. I use these findings to justify visible asset-ID rules and feedback close to the relevant field.

Additional supporting evidence needs careful interpretation. Zeleke and colleagues compared electronic and paper collection through 12 interviewers and 1,246 questionnaires per method. Overall item error rates were 0.60% electronically and 1.67% on paper. Structured inputs and several checks changed together, and the paper comparator differs from this project's browser table. Nakamura and colleagues' 2026 abstract reports 20 therapists in a randomized crossover comparison. Their tested wizard had shorter entry times, fewer errors and lower workload. Navigation and input methods also differed, and the authors reject a universal wizard advantage. None of these papers directly proves better image–metadata matching in this application.

SLIDE 6: Validation revision and hypotheses
Target: 60 seconds. Main talk target 770 seconds (12 minutes 50 seconds).

The remote revision delays initial metadata errors until Save while keeping the rules visible and corrective feedback beside each field. A 2007 study found fewer repeated errors with submission-time feedback than with blur feedback, but its artificial tasks limit generalization. This is therefore a design choice to test. The strongest expectations are fewer missing values and format errors. Accuracy, completion time and SUS remain empirical questions. New sessions carry a fixed UI revision, and existing sessions keep their original behavior. The study analyzes these revisions separately. Frozen desktop v2 retains its historical blur behavior.

Additional panel answers
The remote revision responds to mixed evidence on validation timing. Bargas-Avila and colleagues' 2007 paper reports two studies with 77 and 90 participants. In the second study, mean repeated errors were 3.48 with all errors at submission, 3.17 with one error at a time after submission, and 7.25 with onBlur feedback. The overall difference was significant at p<.03, while completion-time differences were not significant. The artificial tasks used undisclosed formatting obstacles, so this is a reason to test less interruptive initial feedback rather than a universal rule.

The implemented remote revision delays first metadata validation until Save. It retains visible explicit rules, required markers, nearby corrective feedback, review and recovery. A Save attempt can therefore identify problems without interrupting the first pass through the fields. No radio-control conversion is part of this revision. Frozen desktop v2 retains its original blur behavior.

The protocol name remains HLL04-remote-table-v3. New sessions carry the immutable ui_revision value research-20261026-r1. An existing session without a value belongs to legacy-v3 and keeps the old behavior. Exported revision provenance and analysis selection keep these groups separate. Local verification supports the implemented revision behavior. The revision was deployed on 26 October as Amplify job 6; detailed hosted verification is recorded in docs/research-ui-change-notes.md.

The primary expectations are higher completeness and fewer format errors than the browser-table condition. Factual accuracy and image–metadata matching need separate checks because a valid value can still be wrong. Completion time and SUS may improve or worsen, and the study must report both possibilities. The combined comparison cannot isolate a causal effect of validation timing. Literature supplies rationale and hypotheses, while project-specific human results remain unavailable.

SLIDE 8: Validation and data accuracy
Target: 55 seconds. Main talk target 770 seconds (12 minutes 50 seconds).

SLIDE 9: Recovery from a storage failure
Target: 65 seconds. Main talk target 770 seconds (12 minutes 50 seconds).

The storage-recovery evidence on this slide belongs to frozen v2 on 21 October. The earlier design stored image data in localStorage and could not support the selected workload. The repair uses a transactional IndexedDB snapshot and acknowledges a save after transaction completion. In a deliberately induced abort, one record remained in durable storage while the page held two accepted records and a separate unfinished draft. The recovery ZIP retained that work separately. Retry and reload then restored it. A separate quota override did not produce a failure, so this does not prove natural quota recovery. In remote v3, the new walkthrough retained guided work and table images through refresh, but its task-reference panel reset. Preserving data and preserving the visible context are different requirements.

Point to the warning first. Explain that working memory and persistent storage differ.

SLIDE 13: Remote workflow comparison (v3)
Target: 65 seconds. Main talk target 770 seconds (12 minutes 50 seconds).

The remote human method is a new browser-table comparison. Each permitted adult volunteer uses both conditions in the same sitting on their own desktop Chrome. The server alternates starting order across assignments. Each condition starts with a separate two-minute practice and then six assigned tasks, with a fifteen-minute maximum. Both conditions show the same task cards and image choices, and require explicit image selection. The table uses raw text cells. The guided condition retains validation and review, with the immutable UI revision determining when initial errors appear. Legacy and revised sessions are analyzed separately. Block time includes checking, correction, export and self-reported file inspection, but excludes questionnaire and final-upload time. Normal completion requires a current export and file confirmations. People can also finish incomplete, reach the cap or skip. Missing tasks remain in the twenty-four-cell denominators. The exploratory target is six to eight permitted adults, subject to the required arrangements. No human observations are available for this report. Frozen v2 retains its different folder-and-spreadsheet protocol.

Additional panel answers
Remote v3 changes the baseline from a folder/spreadsheet to an in-browser table, allows different participants to use different computers, and uses self-reported local file checks. It therefore cannot be silently pooled with v2. The server submission status is separate from analysis eligibility. Quality, completed timing and valid SUS each need their own eligibility and paired sample size. The synthetic S004/S005 runs cannot enter participant results. Consent screens do not imply institutional approval.

SLIDE 15: Evaluation measures
Backup slide for questions, outside the main talk.
